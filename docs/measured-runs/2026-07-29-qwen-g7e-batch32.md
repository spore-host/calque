# Measured run — Qwen2.5-1.5B on g7e.2xlarge (RTX PRO 6000), N=1000 with `--batch-size 32`

**Verdict: STAY ON MODAL — more emphatically than the single-stream run.** Micro-batching
worked exactly as intended at the GPU level (**~24× faster per item**), and *still* nothing
crosses: batching cuts the Modal bill too (Modal bills busy GPU-seconds), so a short,
load-dominated job remains Modal's home turf. This is the phase detector working.

This run is also the one that **found the occupancy-metric bug** ([#71](https://github.com/spore-host/calque/issues/71)):
it made inference 24× faster and reported occupancy *falling* from ~45% to **2%**.

> **Rate caveat:** **SPOT** acquisition — `R_a` is a spot rate and the instance is
> interruptible. Not the on-demand headline K.

> **Occupancy scope caveat (#71):** the 2% here is a **`whole_run`** mean, and it is the
> clearest demonstration of why that window is wrong — see "The occupancy artifact" below.
> Not recomputable (samples predate the `ts` field). Runs after #71 report `scope: inference`.

## The occupancy artifact (why 2% is not "the GPU was idle")

Occupancy was computed as busy-GPU-seconds ÷ **whole-run** wall-clock, sampled across the
entire rung. That window is `210 s load + 15 s inference + overhead ≈ 339 s`, of which only
~15 s involved any inference at all. The GPU was *saturated during those 15 s*; it was idle
for the 210 s load, as it must be.

So making inference **faster** shrank the busy numerator against a fixed 210 s denominator
and the reported occupancy **fell**. That is backwards from what the number is supposed to
convey, and it's a measurement bug rather than a property of the workload — fixed in #71 by
averaging over warmd's **inference spans** only, with `enter_seconds` continuing to carry
the load cost separately in the K math.

## Why the verdict is still STAY ON MODAL

Not an artifact — this part is real economics. Batching cuts **both** bills: Modal charges
for busy GPU-seconds, so 24× faster inference dropped Modal at 100k from ~$5.96 to ~$1.86.
Meanwhile AWS still pays for the whole rectangle including the one-time load. For a short,
load-dominated job, Modal's scale-to-zero wins, and "raise occupancy to cross K" was the
wrong framing for this workload.

A genuine CROSS needs **steady-state inference to dominate the load** — much larger N, or a
bigger/slower model — measured with load-excluded occupancy. That is the honest next
experiment, not a knob on this one.

## Caveats

- **Spot rate, interruptible** — see the banner. On-demand g7e capacity was exhausted (#18).
- **Occupancy is `whole_run` (2%)** and not recomputable; do not cite it as GPU fill.
- **Per-item seconds under batching are `batch_wall / batch_size`** by construction — items
  in a batch overlap, so this is an amortized throughput figure, not a single-item latency.
- The `--concurrency` path is **guarded off** for vLLM-offline bodies (not thread-safe); this
  run used `--batch-size`, which is the correct lever. See #68.
